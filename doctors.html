For Doctors

For Afghan physicians living abroad

Your knowledge and experience can help strengthen healthcare in Afghanistan. There are many ways to contribute — a 2–3 month rotation is just one of them.

How you could help

Pick the kind of contribution that works for you

Different doctors have different availability, circumstances, and preferences. We are interested in all of them.

✈

2–3 month clinical rotation

A structured period working alongside healthcare teams in an Afghan partner institution, then returning to your country of residence. This is the model we are designing toward for the future pilot program.

⌚

Shorter medical mission

If a 2–3 month rotation is too long but you can spend a shorter period in Afghanistan at some point, we would still like to hear from you.

✎

Teaching and training

Developing curricula, running short courses, leading workshops, or providing structured clinical teaching for Afghan colleagues — either in person or remotely.

☎

Remote mentorship

Case-based mentorship, regular calls with an Afghan colleague or department, and specialist input on difficult cases from wherever you live.

⚙

Medical equipment support

Helping identify what equipment is actually needed, advising on appropriate technology for the setting, or helping source and ship items.

⌘

Organisation and fundraising

Helping build the initiative itself: governance, legal structure, partnerships, communications, or fundraising. Every medical network needs a backbone.

What happens after you register

Registering your interest ≠ committing to a trip

The registration form is an interest database, not an application for clinical practice. Registering does not oblige you to do anything at all.

Once we have a clearer picture of the network, we will:

  • Share what we learn about specialties, availability, and preferences
  • Ask for your input on program design and practical support
  • Keep you informed as we build hospital partnerships
  • Only approach you specifically when something relevant arises

If, later on, you are selected for a rotation, we will run a proper credential-verification process at that stage — not on the first form.

?

What we will NOT ask you for yet

On the initial registration form you won't be asked to upload a passport, medical license, certificates, CV, or ID documents. Those are important, but they belong to a later stage — once there is a real rotation on the table and a real partner hospital.

✓

We are not recruiting for a mission today

We are developing a program to connect Afghan healthcare professionals abroad with healthcare institutions in Afghanistan. Once we are ready for a pilot, we will reach out to the network directly.

Your questions

Questions we want to ask the first 100 doctors

The point of building the network first is to learn. These are some of the questions whose answers will actually shape the program.

  • Which specialties are available in the diaspora?
  • How many would consider going to Afghanistan?
  • What would prevent you from going?
  • What duration would you accept?
  • What support would you need?
  • Which Afghan hospitals would you trust?
  • What medical equipment do you believe is most urgently needed?
