Why this matters

A broader network creates a stronger program

A physician can only work well if the rest of the health system works well too. A future rotation program that only includes doctors would miss — literally and metaphorically — the rest of the team.

Building a broader healthcare diaspora from day one gives us:

  • A richer pool of expertise for partner institutions to draw on
  • Better design for training programs and equipment planning
  • A larger community that can grow over time
  • The ability to assemble truly multidisciplinary teams later
What to expect: Registering your interest is not an application or a commitment. We are building a broad network first, then designing concrete opportunities (rotations, remote teaching, equipment support, and more) with the people in it.

Ways you could contribute

Just like doctors, different professionals can contribute in different ways depending on availability and preference.

In-person support

Joining a future short or longer stay, working alongside teams on the ground.

Training and teaching

Helping develop the skills and confidence of colleagues in Afghanistan.

Remote support

Case discussions, protocol development, or mentorship from your country of residence.

Equipment and systems

Advising on what tools, systems, and devices are actually appropriate and usable.

Program design

Helping us design the program itself so it works for the disciplines involved.

Connections

Helping us reach other Afghan healthcare professionals you know abroad.
