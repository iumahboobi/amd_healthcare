Hospitals & Partners

For hospitals, institutions, and partners

Strong partnerships on both sides — in Afghanistan and in the countries where the diaspora lives — are what will make a future program work.

Initial focus

Starting with Kandahar, then expanding carefully

To keep quality and supervision high, the program will begin with a narrow geographic focus before any broader expansion.

◉

Afghan hospital partners

We are looking for Afghan healthcare institutions that can provide a safe, well-supervised clinical environment for visiting professionals and that have clear, identified needs we can actually help with. The initial focus is on Kandahar.

⌂

International partners and supporters

Institutions abroad, medical associations, diaspora organisations, funders, and logistics partners whose support could make the program more credible, sustainable, and safe.

What a partner hospital could expect

A small, careful pilot — not a flood of visitors

Any initial engagement would be modest in scope and designed to be useful rather than busy.

  • A carefully selected, small number of professionals per initial site
  • Clear scope of work agreed in advance with hospital leadership
  • Focus on training, mentorship, and knowledge transfer alongside service
  • Proper credential verification before any clinical placement
  • Close communication throughout and a thorough review afterwards

Nothing will be rushed. The goal of a pilot is to learn what works — not to claim scale.

Partner principles

How we want to work with institutions

☺

Hospital-led needs first

We start from what the partner hospital identifies as real needs — not from what volunteers happen to want to do.

◎

Long-term orientation

We are interested in durable relationships, not one-off visits that disappear after a few weeks.

★

Quality and safety first

Proper credentials, clear scopes of practice, and adequate supervision are non-negotiable.

↔

Transfer, not replace

Preference for activities that build local capacity rather than replacing existing work.

☰

Transparent evaluation

Every pilot activity will be reviewed honestly with partners before any expansion is discussed.

⚖

Fair and compliant

Structured to respect relevant legal, ethical, and data-protection requirements on all sides — including GDPR.

Next steps for partners

What a first conversation would cover

We don't believe in cold proposals. An initial conversation is more like a structured discovery:

  • What are the hospital's current priority needs and staffing gaps?
  • Which specialties, cadres, or skills would be the most useful in a volunteer?
  • What supervision, accommodation, security, and onboarding can the hospital provide?
  • How would the institution prefer to measure success of a pilot?
  • What kind of timeframes and commitments are realistic on both sides?

There's nothing to sign on day one. We'd rather understand the reality first — then propose something small enough to evaluate honestly.
