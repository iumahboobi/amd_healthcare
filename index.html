Afghan Medical Diaspora

Your expertise can help strengthen healthcare in Afghanistan.

We are building a network of Afghan doctors and healthcare professionals living abroad who are interested in contributing their knowledge, experience and time to healthcare in Afghanistan.

Our vision is to develop voluntary, structured medical rotations in which qualified Afghan physicians can spend approximately 2–3 months working alongside healthcare professionals in Afghanistan, before returning to their country of residence.

Our Vision

Connecting expertise abroad with healthcare in Afghanistan

The project aims to connect the Afghan medical diaspora with healthcare institutions in Afghanistan through a range of voluntary contributions.

+

Temporary clinical service

Structured 2–3 month rotations supporting teams in Afghan healthcare institutions alongside local professionals.

★

Specialist expertise

Deep specialist knowledge shared where it is most needed, on both clinical and advisory levels.

✎

Training and mentorship

Hands-on training, case supervision, and long-term mentorship for Afghan colleagues.

↔

Knowledge transfer

Exchanges of protocols, guidelines, clinical reasoning, and public health practice.

⚙

Medical equipment

Helping identify, source, and deploy the equipment most needed by Afghan healthcare facilities.

⌘

Long-term professional connections

Building enduring relationships between professionals abroad and in Afghanistan.

For Healthcare Professionals
The questions we want to ask the network

These answers will actually shape the program

Getting the first 100 people into the network is Phase 1 for a reason. Everything about the program — durations, locations, support, partner hospitals — will be designed with what we learn from you.

Which specialties are available in the diaspora?

Understanding the network's strengths lets us match real needs to real expertise.

How many would consider going to Afghanistan?

A realistic picture of interest and availability shapes every other decision.

What would prevent you from going?

Logistics, family, work, security, insurance — we want to design around real barriers.

What duration would you accept?

2–3 months is a starting point. If the network suggests something else, we'll adapt.

What support would you need?

Flights, accommodation, security, insurance, local logistics, briefing — tell us.

Which Afghan hospitals would you trust?

Credible partners come from the network's lived knowledge, not a random list.

What medical equipment is most urgently needed?

Clinicians with on-the-ground context know best what's usable and what's not.

Which regions besides Kandahar matter?

Initial focus is Kandahar, but the network's input will guide thoughtful expansion later.
