Our Mission

Strengthening healthcare in Afghanistan, together

We are developing a program to connect Afghan healthcare professionals abroad with healthcare institutions in Afghanistan — starting with a network, not promises.

What we aim to do

Six ways the diaspora can contribute

Not every contribution has to be a trip to Afghanistan. Depending on your availability, specialty, and preferences, there are several meaningful ways to support healthcare in Afghanistan.

1

Temporary clinical service

Voluntary 2–3 month clinical rotations alongside local teams in partner Afghan healthcare institutions.

2

Specialist expertise

Consultative input and case review for complex conditions that are difficult to manage locally.

3

Training and mentorship

Structured teaching, hands-on training, and peer mentorship for Afghan colleagues.

4

Knowledge transfer

Helping develop guidelines, protocols, and clinical standards with partner institutions.

5

Medical equipment

Helping identify equipment needs, source appropriate items, and support installation and training.

6

Long-term professional connections

Enduring relationships between professionals and institutions that outlast any single trip.

Our approach

Start small. Learn fast. Expand carefully.

Our initial goal is to establish a small, carefully managed pilot program and expand based on its results — not to promise large-scale operations from day one.

We do not yet claim to send doctors to Afghanistan, to cover flights and accommodation, or to treat thousands of patients freely. Instead, we are building the community, the partnerships, and the structures that could one day make a program like that credible and safe.

  • Build a network of interested healthcare professionals first
  • Design the program with doctors, not on our own
  • Identify trusted Afghan hospital partners
  • Run a small pilot and learn from it
Full transparency: We are currently in the development and network-building phase. We are looking for Afghan medical professionals abroad who want to help shape the program and potentially participate in future rotations. If that sounds like you — or if you simply want to follow progress — the easiest way to stay involved is to join the network.

Our planned path

Phase 1 — Network building

Collect interest from Afghan doctors and healthcare professionals living abroad. Understand availability, specialties, and willingness to contribute.

Phase 2 — Co-design

Ask the network what would make participation realistic, which hospitals they trust, and what support is required before, during, and after a rotation.

Phase 3 — Partnerships

Identify and carefully select initial hospital partners in Afghanistan, starting with a focus on Kandahar.

Phase 4 — Pilot program

Run a small, closely supervised pilot rotation and evaluate it thoroughly. Learn, adjust, and only then consider expanding.

Guiding principles

Six principles we will not compromise on

Hospital-led needs first

We start from what partner hospitals identify as real needs — not from what volunteers happen to want to do.

Long-term orientation

We are interested in durable relationships, not one-off visits that disappear after a few weeks.

Quality and safety first

Proper credentials, clear scopes of practice, and adequate supervision are non-negotiable.

Transfer, not replace

Preference for activities that build local capacity rather than replacing existing work.

Transparent evaluation

Every pilot activity will be reviewed honestly with partners before any expansion is discussed.

Fair and compliant

Structured to respect relevant legal, ethical, and data-protection requirements on all sides — including GDPR.
